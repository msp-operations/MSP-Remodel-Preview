Safiyah Mohamed
PhD in Cancer Science · University of Oxford
MSP 2022 United Kingdom Cancer science

My journey with the Maastricht Science Programme began in February 2020. Just a few weeks later, the programme moved online due to the pandemic. This made my experience quite different from what I originally expected. Despite this, I still look back at my time at MSP very positively.

One of the things I valued most was the freedom the programme offered. MSP allowed me to explore my interests while still receiving guidance from an academic advisor. This helped me feel confident in making decisions about my future. The skills courses and project work were especially important for me. They helped me build practical skills that later became essential in my academic journey and beyond.

"Do not be afraid to ask for help or advice from staff, students, or alumni. If you do not ask, you will not know."

I also really enjoyed being involved in the MSP community outside of coursework. I was a student ambassador and contributed to MSP's social media. Being involved in this way made me feel connected to the programme and gave me insight into how things worked behind the scenes. MSP felt like a small and supportive community rather than just a degree.

After graduating with a major in Neuroscience, I moved to Amsterdam where I completed a two-year master's degree in Neurobiology. I then took a gap year from academia and I am now pursuing a PhD in Cancer Science at the University of Oxford.

My advice to current MSP students is to plan ahead and think about how your choices align with your long-term goals. Do not be afraid to ask for help or advice from staff, students, or alumni. Being open and asking questions is important. If you do not ask, you will not know. Most importantly, enjoy your time at MSP. The small class sizes, supportive staff, and sense of community are special and not something you easily find again.

Silvio Bonni
PhD in Synthetic Biology · University of Auckland
MSP 2021 New Zealand Synthetic biology

Hi, Hallo, Kia ora!

My name is Silvio, I'm originally from Germany, and I joined MSP in 2018. It was an incredibly formative time for me: I found my passion for science, made lifelong friends, and felt truly supported by our close-knit community in small yet lively Maastricht.

During MSP, I discovered my interest in synthetic biology, did several projects with my (amazing) lecturer/supervisor/mentor Erik, and even took part in the international iGEM competition, which was a huge highlight and really shaped where I ended up going next.

After graduating, I worked as a lab technician for a bit, but I quickly realised I wasn't done with research. So I went back for a Master's in Cell Biology at VU Amsterdam. Part of that took me all the way to Japan, to Okinawa Institute of Science and Technology, where I worked with Ben, one of my supervisors from MSP (and now good friend). Funny how those connections stick with you.

"Funny how those connections stick with you."

I still wasn't ready to stop exploring, so I packed my bags again and moved to New Zealand. I'm now doing a PhD in synthetic biology at the University of Auckland, using genetically modified yeast to answer questions in evolutionary biology. New Zealand is stunning, my photo is from a recent hike showcasing the turquoise thermal pools of Te Papa-Kura-o-Taranaki (Mount Taranaki!). Yet I feel like I've only just started discovering it.

Looking back, MSP opened so many doors for me, it's honestly wild how far it's taken me. I'm really grateful for that time and would absolutely recommend MSP to anyone thinking about joining.

Sophia Aristidou
Young Professional Patent Examiner · European Patent Office, Munich
MSP 2023 Germany Intellectual property

I graduated from MSP in 2023 with a focus on biology. While I enjoyed studying life sciences, I gradually realised that lab work wasn't for me. MSP gave me the freedom to discover that early on and the confidence to change direction without feeling limited by my degree.

"MSP gave me the freedom to discover that early on and the confidence to change direction."

After graduation, I stayed in Maastricht to pursue a master's in intellectual property law, combining my scientific background with legal and analytical skills. This interdisciplinary step turned out to be a great fit and showed me how valuable an MSP education can be beyond traditional science careers.

Since September 2024, I've been working in Munich at the European Patent Office. My work lets me stay close to innovation and research, without being in the lab myself. I enjoy the international environment, the intellectual challenge, and the feeling that my background in science still matters in my current role.

My advice to current MSP students is to stay open-minded. It's okay if your interests shift along the way. Your degree doesn't lock you into one career, it can open doors you didn't even know existed :)

Dora Bobanović
Sustainability Specialist Non-Food · ALDI SÜD Holding, Salzburg
MSP 2022 Austria Sustainability

When I started MSP, I had no idea which field or industry I might want to work in. I really enjoyed the program's multidisciplinary approach, as I had always struggled to narrow my interests down to just one field. For me, MSP was the perfect way to explore a wide range of subjects and take classes across very different disciplines (from astrophysics to art conservation to philosophy).

Through courses related to climate and sustainability, I developed a strong passion for sustainable development. This led me to pursue a master's degree in International Development at IE University in Madrid, where I gained a more social and economic perspective on sustainability. After graduating, I joined the ALDI South headquarters in Salzburg, working on sustainability implementation for non-food products.

A professor at MSP once told me that MSP students develop skills that truly prepare them for the real world, and I can say with confidence that this is 100% true. During our project periods, we constantly had to present complex topics in a simple and accessible way, so that even someone with no prior exposure to the subject could understand. In other words, we learned how to adapt our narrative to our audience.

To this day, this remains one of the most valuable skills I bring to the workplace: the ability to engage stakeholders by communicating in a way that is clear, relevant, and meaningful to them.

"You only value what you've learned once you step into the real world and see that you see things differently compared to others."

Jaidev Dhavle
Associate Programme Officer · IRENA, Bonn
MSP 2014 Germany Renewable energy

At MSP when I started in 2011, I wasn't sure how my life would pan out and what I wanted to pursue academically. I took a chance with this new programme in the liberal arts and sciences. 3 years at the programme changed me as a person and showed me that opportunities are always there if the hard work and dedication is put in! My classmates were inspirational and I found a liking to chemistry during my time at the programme.

"3 years at the programme changed me as a person."

During my masters, while I enjoyed the sciences, I came to the realisation that I wasn't cut out for the PhD path/life. Hence, I decided to follow in the footsteps of my family and pursue a career at an international organization. I'm currently working with the International Renewable Energy Agency where I am supporting countries to uptake renewable energy technologies across different sectors.

My advice to future students would be to really take your time to find what you want to study, bond with your classmates, and make the most of your time at the university. MSP has a very unique atmosphere that is not found anywhere else!

Francesca Diaz Cabal
Gray whale researcher · Ecuador / Mexico
MSP graduate Ecuador / Mexico Marine biology

Hello everyone! My name is Francesca, and I'm from Ecuador. Ever since I was five years old, I knew I wanted to become a marine biologist and study whales. I remember when I was applying for different universities, and MSP immediately stood out because of the BTR programme it offered. The fact that it allowed students to do their bachelor thesis research anywhere in the world was truly the tip of the iceberg for me, and the final push that brought me to Maastricht.

My university years in the Netherlands were amazing. Living in such an international environment, choosing my own courses, biking everywhere, and meeting people from all over the world became some of the highlights of my studies. MSP gave me the freedom to shape my academic path around my passions, and that made all the difference. As my Bachelor Thesis Research (BTR) approached, I decided to dream big and that took me to Baja California Sur, Mexico, to work with photo identification with gray whales.

"My family likes to joke that I 'work where the whales go,' and honestly, that's the truth."

For my thesis, I joined PRIMMA (Programa de Investigación de Mamíferos Marinos), and what started as a research project quickly became so much more. I was invited to be part of the gray whale research team, and to this day, I'm still working with them. Since my thesis, I've been invited back to Mexico for the gray whale season from January to April — twice already, and I'll be returning again for the 2026 season, which will be my third.

Thanks to my BTR on gray whales the doors in the marine mammal community opened for me, and I've been able to explore other opportunities. I recently finished my first humpback whale season (June–December) as a volunteer with the Pacific Whale Foundation in Ecuador, where I spent six months as part of the research team. I joined expeditions, worked on photo-ID, and learned new research techniques that I'm excited to apply in my future projects. Now, as I finish the year in Puerto Lopez, Ecuador, I'll be heading back to Mexico to work with gray whales once again.

Looking ahead, my goal is to pursue a master's degree using data from Ecuador's marine mammal team, where there is still so much to be discovered about these migrating species. I'm hoping to find a programme that, just like MSP, allows me to do my thesis abroad and continue combining research, travel, and real-world experience.

"Dream big and stay persistent. If you truly want something and work for it, it will come."

Vitesh
Nuclear researcher · Singapore
MSP graduate Singapore Nuclear engineering

The mysteries of the universe — from planets and stars to black holes and galaxies — have always fascinated me. My name is Vitesh, though many know me as Vitz, and I have long held a deep passion for physics.

My time at the Maastricht Science Programme stands out as one of the most meaningful and enriching educational experiences of my life. I am truly honoured to have been part of the programme. While the courses themselves were intellectually rigorous and engaging, it was the educational approach and the dedication of the lecturers that made MSP truly exceptional.

"I could have studied similar subjects elsewhere, but the learning environment at MSP was unlike anything I had previously experienced."

It nurtured curiosity, encouraged independent thinking, and most importantly, reignited my genuine enjoyment of learning. Following my studies at MSP, I was awarded a scholarship in Singapore to further my education, where I pursued Nuclear Engineering.

Today, I work as a Nuclear Researcher in Singapore, assessing the safety systems of various reactor technologies, conducting severe accident and neutronic simulations, and performing radiological environmental impact assessments.

I can say with confidence that the curriculum at MSP, together with the guidance and inspiration provided by my lecturers — particularly Dr. Chris Pawley, Dr. Sarah Williams, Dr. Ronald Westra, and Dr. Donna Carroll, among many others — and the support of my course mates, shaped my academic journey in profound ways. The experience was not only unforgettable, but also instrumental in moulding me into the passionate and inquisitive researcher I am today.

"The important thing is not to stop questioning. Curiosity has its own reason for existence." — Albert Einstein

Daria Legostaeva
Environmental engineer at SBM Offshore · Netherlands
MSP 2024 Netherlands Biochemistry SBM Offshore

If you had asked me back then, I wouldn't have predicted that I would end up at MSP for my bachelor's. At the time, I had never been to Maastricht or toured the university and the FSE faculty. In high school, I knew I wanted to pursue natural sciences, but I couldn't commit to just one subject. The diversity and flexibility of the MSP programme were what truly drew me to apply.

Looking back at my journey, I didn't expect my studies to lead me to where I am today, and I mean that in the best way possible. Early on, I realized that a purely academic or research path was not entirely for me. I was much more drawn to the application of knowledge in industry and scientific business practices.

"I wanted to become a bridge between scientific research and real-world business applications."

After completing my BTR at the Brightlands campus with an innovative startup, I became deeply interested in sustainability and environmental topics. That experience helped me realize this was the market I wanted to pursue.

Being an international student in Europe comes with its own pressures: meeting grade requirements to retain residency, and after graduation, facing the ticking clock of finding a master's programme or a job with visa sponsorship. I found myself in a position where no particular master's programme sparked strong interest, and I felt ready to give working life a chance — almost as a trial to confirm whether sustainability and environmental science were truly the fields I wanted to grow in.

The journey to finding my first job was definitely not easy. At times it was disappointing, lonely, and frustrating. What kept me going was my desire to stay in the Netherlands, which had become a new home with its routines, friendships, and sense of familiarity.

After countless applications, interviews, rejections, and unanswered emails, I connected with a recruiter from my current company. That opportunity led to an offer for a position as an Environmental Engineer. Coming from a non-traditional engineering background, the first year came with a steep learning curve. However, I kept reminding myself that throughout my education, it was precisely the skills of research, self-study, and proactivity that allowed me to grow.

This February, I completed my first year working in a large corporate environment and living in a new city. It has been a year of incredible highs, but also moments of doubt and uncertainty.

"Every path is unique. If you are truly committed to something, it will find its way to you. Patience and timing are always part of the journey."

Yannick
Wildlife crime consultant · Fauna Flux
MSP graduate Copenhagen / Belize / Ecuador Wildlife crime Conservation

After completing my bachelor's at MSP, I went to do a master's at the University of Copenhagen. The programme offered much flexibility and I exploited this.

I conducted a research project in the Llanganates-Sangay ecological corridor in Ecuador where I worked on some conservation genetics specifically tailored to herpetofauna, and a year and a half later, I went to Belize to do my master's thesis on the American Crocodile together with the Crocodile Research Coalition funded by the IUCN Crocodile Specialist Group and the Hendrik Muller Fonds.

"I wanted to do something that would help create change now. This led me to the field of wildlife crime."

After my studies, I decided to change direction and pursue something else to research. I wanted to do something that would help create change now. This led me to the field of wildlife crime.

Currently, I work as a consultant at Langland Conservation through my own company Fauna Flux. It is an exciting company that leverages the power of technology and military experience to combat the illegal trade of wildlife. We help local partners optimise their conservation efforts to achieve actionable results by supporting them with analytics and intelligence.
